This review separates three things that must remain separate in research: what the selected workbooks actually contain, when a policy applied, and what a researcher can infer from an institution's missing cell. A disappearing header does not establish zero aid. An unchanged header does not establish a stable eligible population or a stable institutional reporting unit.

The review covers every observed measure-family addition, removal and split in all **86 selected cached workbooks**, representing grants and Direct Loans for AY1999–2000 through AY2024–2025, FFEL through AY2009–2010, and campus programs for AY2001–2002 through AY2023–2024. It is exhaustive for those observed schema transitions. It is a source-backed register of major policy changes relevant to longitudinal interpretation, rather than a claim to enumerate every statutory amendment, annual appropriation, institution-specific exception or student eligibility rule. Future policy outside the selected award years is not applied to historical data.

The reproducible evidence consists of:

- [Exact source-header inventory](../Metadata/source_schema_inventory.csv): 1,515 header records with award year, source URL, SHA-256, sheet, original label, canonical measure and observed symbol tokens.
- [Workbook definitions](evidence/selected_workbook_schema_definitions.json): titles, run-date preambles, 76 workbooks with available definition sheets and four explicit campus footer notes. Ten selected workbooks have no separate definition sheet; older definitions are interpretation evidence, not invented quotations from newer workbooks.
- [Policy/event register](../Metadata/program_policy_events.csv): 48 events, with source URLs, publication dates when available, legal effective dates where known, coarse award-year annotations, confidence, and coding implications.
- [Measure definitions](../Metadata/measure_definitions.json): all 64 distinct source measure names, exact observed year sets by family, units, counting caveats and linked policy events. Year sets preserve gaps rather than implying continuous availability from a minimum and maximum.
- [Compact source evidence](evidence/policy_source_evidence.json): the verified claim and source for each event. Blank publication dates mean a dated page was not established; they are not fabricated dates.

`Scripts/fsa_policy.py` exposes these metadata for dictionaries. `policy_registry_hash()` hashes the three machine-readable inputs. `metadata_for_measure(column)` returns scalar and JSON-text fields; the loan computation module supplies actual derived formulas. These functions never impute or alter an observed value. Policy effective dates and approximate affected award years are separate because legal rules can attach to a loan-period start, first disbursement, application, credit check, or individual status.

**Pell comparability within a continuous column**

Pell recipient counts and dollar totals do not measure a fixed eligibility regime. The 2009–10 and 2010–11 two-Scheduled-Award regime allowed up to 200%; it was repealed for 2011–12. Year-Round Pell returned in 2017–18 with a 150% ceiling. These are distinct regimes. [Two-award guidance](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/dear-colleague-letters/2010-08-03/gen-10-14-subject-implementation-two-federal-pell-grants-one-award-year), [2011 repeal](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/fsa-print/publication/2803), [2017 restoration](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/dear-colleague-letters/2017-06-19/gen-17-06-subject-implementation-year-round-pell-grants).

The 600% lifetime limit began in 2012–13. The same period also changed the automatic-zero EFC threshold to $23,000 and restricted ability-to-benefit entry routes. Career-pathway eligibility was subsequently restored under specified conditions, then revised. These changes can move recipient counts while the Pell column remains identical. [Lifetime limit](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/fsa-print/publication/2961), [2012 EFC change](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/fsa-print/publication/1083), [2012 Title IV changes](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/fsa-print/publication/2912), [historical career-pathway guidance](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/fsa-print/publication/2986).

The 2017–18 FAFSA began using prior-prior tax-year income; both 2016–17 and 2017–18 therefore used 2015 income. Income covariates should use a documented lag rather than assume the award year and measured family income coincide. [FSA guidance](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/fsa-print/publication/3067).

Later changes include Pell eligibility restoration following certain closures, Children of Fallen Heroes treatment inside Pell from 2018–19, removal of drug-conviction and Selective Service aid restrictions starting 2021–22, and expanded Pell access for qualifying prison education programs from July 2023. Some earlier experimental-site Pell access existed, so July 2023 is not proof that all pre-2023 prison-related Pell was absent. [Closed-school restoration](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/electronic-announcements/2017-04-03/grants-subject-guidance-cod-processing-pell-grant-restoration-students-who-attended-closed-schools), [Children of Fallen Heroes](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/fsa-print/publication/9300), [2021 eligibility implementation](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/fsa-print/publication/1003303), [Prison Education Programs](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/topics/prison-education-programs).

AY2024–25 introduces an especially large interpretation break: SAI replaces EFC; maximum/minimum Pell pathways use income/poverty and family circumstances; continuous enrollment intensity replaces the older part-time bands; and the additional Year-Round Pell portion no longer requires half-time enrollment. IASG/Special Rule eligibility also moves into Pell. These changes cannot be removed with a column rename or one adjustment factor. [GEN-23-11](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/fsa-print/publication/1005218), [2024–25 SAI and Pell eligibility](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/fsa-handbook/2024-2025/application-and-verification-guide/ch3-student-aid-index-sai-and-pell-grant-eligibility).

Annual maximums, EFC/SAI schedules, appropriations and nominal prices also vary. For example, the scheduled Pell maximum increased by $500 between 2022–23 and 2023–24. The panel preserves nominal source dollars; it is not inflation-adjusted and the policy register is not a complete numeric annual award-schedule series. A study attributing changes in dollars per recipient to institutional behavior should account for these annual parameters and changing recipient mix. [2023–24 implementation](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/electronic-announcements/2023-03-07/operational-implementation-guidance-federal-student-aids-implementation-2023-24-federal-pell-grant-payment-and-disbursement-schedules).

**Campus-based programs: allocation, student payments and changing match rules**

The workbook definition calls `Federal Award` the federal contribution to the school/program and `Disbursements` actual school-reported disbursements. They are not interchangeable and should never be added together. FISAP program summaries include federal funding, authorized transfers and nonfederal matching shares in total FSEOG/FWS student expenditures. FWS denotes compensation for work rather than a Pell-like entitlement. Perkins lending historically recycled repayments and institutional contributions as well as federal capital. [2008–09 FISAP Instructions, Part VI](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/sites/default/files/attachments/fisapformandinst/0809FISAPInstructions.pdf), [campus program accounting](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/fsa-handbook/2024-2025/vol6/ch1-campus-based-programs-common-elements).

This supports treating campus disbursements as program-level student payments potentially containing federal and nonfederal resources, not a pure federal-expenditure measure. An exact mapping from each vintage of the published volume field to an individual FISAP line was not provided in every source. Therefore, the pipeline does not mechanically derive a federal share by multiplying disbursements by 75%, or infer nonfederal expenditure from the difference between disbursements and the initial federal allocation. Transfers, carry-forward/back authority, unspent funds, administrative allowances and institution-specific match waivers further prevent that shortcut.

The CARES Act changed this relationship: institutional match waivers applied to 2019–20 and 2020–21, with a for-profit-employer FWS exception. Emergency authority allowed unspent FWS transfers to FSEOG and relaxed ordinary FSEOG eligibility for emergency grants. These are substantive comparability changes despite continuous FWS/FSEOG columns. The matching waiver did not continue into 2021–22. [2020 deadline guidance](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/fsa-print/publication/37852), [FWS emergency provisions](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/fsa-handbook/2020-2021/vol6/ch2-federal-work-study-program), [2022–23 handbook confirmation](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/fsa-handbook/2022-2023/vol6/ch1-campus-based-programs-common-elements).

No new Perkins capital was authorized after 2004–05, but existing revolving funds continued lending. New-loan authorization ended September 30, 2017 and subsequent disbursements ended June 30, 2018. Servicing and FISAP reporting of the portfolio continued. The source's later removal of volume columns therefore does not describe liquidation of all outstanding Perkins debt. [Perkins FAQs](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/fsa-print/faq/1002598), [Perkins participation and wind-down](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/fsa-handbook/2024-2025/vol6/ch3-participating-perkins-loan-program).

**Loan channels, borrower types and dates**

The full loan reconstruction rules are in [loan harmonization](loan_harmonization.md). Subsidized, unsubsidized and PLUS are different loan categories; Direct and FFEL are delivery channels; parent and graduate PLUS refer to different borrowing purposes; and undergraduate/graduate splits are not consistently available in the early years. Consolidation loans are explicitly excluded by the historical workbook definitions. Combining Direct/FFEL columns is a data aggregation, not a debt-consolidation measure.

The Grad PLUS boundary is a useful example of why legal dates must not overwrite data. Loans became available from July 2006, yet the Direct operational announcement explicitly discusses processing 2005–06 Grad PLUS. The sources classify a loan by its loan-period start; later disbursements remain associated with that loan cohort. Observed 2005–06 Graduate PLUS amounts are therefore retained. The Direct announcement is direct evidence for Direct processing; the FFEL interpretation also relies on its own workbook loan-period definition. [June 2006 operational guidance](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/electronic-announcements/2006-06-23/summary-hera-operational-implementation-guidance-cps-cod-system-and-edexpress-suite-updated-guidance-direct-plus-loan-graduate-and-professional-students-supersedes-5162006-electronic).

The FFEL cutoff concerns first disbursements, with valid subsequent disbursements allowed later. Direct graduate subsidized eligibility instead ended for loan periods beginning on or after July 1, 2012. These different clocks preclude a universal rule based only on the first four digits of an award-year label. [FFEL deadline guidance](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/fsa-print/publication/2775), [graduate subsidized eligibility](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/electronic-announcements/2012-07-10/direct-loans-subject-direct-loan-interest-rates-and-changes-student-eligibility-direct-subsidized-loans).

Additional volume-relevant changes include 2007 and 2008 borrowing-limit increases, the 150% subsidized usage restriction from 2013 and its 2021 repeal, and changed PLUS credit standards from March 2015. Their effects are not measurable borrower by borrower in this institution-level aggregate. [2007 limits](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/historical-resources/2007-04-12/summary-annual-loan-limit-increases-certain-direct-subsidized-loan-and-direct-unsubsidized-loan-borrowers), [2008 changes](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/fsa-print/publication/2669), [subsidized usage limits](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/topics/150-percent-direct-subsidized-loan-limit-information), [repeal FAQs](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/fsa-print/faq/1003333), [PLUS credit standards](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/electronic-announcements/2015-01-27/general-subject-early-implementation-changes-regulations-adverse-credit-history-under-direct-plus-loan-program).

Sequestration affected TEACH and IASG awards, campus appropriations and Direct loan fees with different effective clocks; Pell itself was exempt. Initial 2013 published reductions were revised and schools were directed to correct records. Do not apply an old announced reduction rate to all annual disbursements, and do not confuse gross loan principal, origination commitment, borrower net proceeds and outstanding debt. [Initial scope](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/electronic-announcements/2013-03-01/ope-announcements-subject-impact-sequestration-title-iv-student-financial-assistance-programs), [corrected 2013 grant rates](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/electronic-announcements/2013-05-31/grants-subject-important-update-teach-grant-further-changes-sequestration-required-reductions).

**Counting, missingness and release vintage**

Recipient counts are program-specific. Parent PLUS recipients denote students supported by parent loans, not a count of parent borrower accounts. Program/channel counts can overlap, and students can appear at different institutions or in multiple years. They cannot be summed into unique people. When a derived recipient sum is useful, its name must say it is a count sum and its scope must remain visible. Bounds require defensible component bounds; a partial sum is not a complete total. [FSA's report interpretation guidance](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/electronic-announcements/2026-03-13/federal-student-aid-posts-updated-reports-fsa-data-center).

Explicit numeric zero, blank source cell, literal dash, suppressed count, privacy-redacted dollar cell, unavailable header, unavailable family report, and an institution absent from a report are different states. For literal `<10`, a nonnegative integer count provides a conservative interval from 0 to 9. The separate publication of zero cells suggests—but does not by itself prove—a lower bound of 1, so the metadata do not silently assert that stronger bound. A redacted dollar cell has no automatically inferred dollar value from the recipient limit.

The annual grant/loan output selects the cumulative award-year Q4 worksheet once. Adding Q1 through Q4 cumulative worksheets would double count. Full-year coverage is not the same as a final vintage: source definitions describe refreshing at least seven previous quarters; actual run dates differ across files. The latest selected 2024–25 grant/Direct files state data as of January 1, 2026. This review verifies the cached source hashes and their internal dates; it does not certify that the cached inventory equals every latest official release.

Remaining interpretation limits are explicit: dash semantics; the exact reason for the 2016 Perkins allocation-column removal; the precise publication-policy origin of 2021 suppression; full all-year volume-to-FISAP line lineage; uniform later-workbook definition continuity where definition sheets are absent; and a complete numeric annual policy-parameter series. These limits do not require dropping valid data. They require retained source evidence, missingness states, conservative aggregates and a research design that states its population, time alignment and policy regime.
